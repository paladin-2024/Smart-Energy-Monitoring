## 1. Prerequisites

- A modern web browser (Chrome or Firefox recommended)
- A free Wokwi account — sign up at [wokwi.com](https://wokwi.com)
- The project files from this repository:
  - `Simulation/sketch.ino`
  - `Simulation/diagram.json`

No software installation required. Wokwi runs entirely in the browser.

---

## 2. Creating a New Wokwi Project

**Step 1:** Open your browser and navigate to [wokwi.com](https://wokwi.com).

**Step 2:** Click **"Sign In"** (top right) and log in with your account.

**Step 3:** Click the **"+"** button or **"New Project"** from the dashboard.

**Step 4:** In the project type selector, choose **"ESP32"** → **"ESP32 DevKit V1"**.
> *Screenshot description: You will see a grid of starter project templates. The ESP32 DevKit V1 card shows an image of the ESP32 board. Click it.*

**Step 5:** Wokwi opens the editor with a default sketch and a bare ESP32 on the canvas. You will replace both files in the next steps.

---

## 3. Loading the Circuit (diagram.json)

**Step 1:** In the Wokwi editor, locate the file tabs at the top of the left panel. You will see `sketch.ino` and `diagram.json`.

**Step 2:** Click the **`diagram.json`** tab.

**Step 3:** Select all existing content in the editor (`Ctrl+A` / `Cmd+A`) and delete it.

**Step 4:** Open `Simulation/diagram.json` from this repository in any text editor, copy the entire contents, and paste it into the Wokwi `diagram.json` editor.

**Step 5:** Press **`Ctrl+S`** (or **`Cmd+S`** on Mac) to save.

**Step 6:** Click the canvas tab — you should now see the following components appear:
- ESP32 DevKit V1 (centre)
- SSD1306 OLED display (top left)
- Two potentiometers (left side)
- Green LED with resistor (right side)
- Red LED with resistor (right side)
- Active buzzer (right side)

> *Screenshot description: The canvas shows all 9 components wired together with coloured connection lines. The OLED is connected with blue and green wires (SCL/SDA). Potentiometers have yellow and orange wires to the ESP32.*

## 4. Loading the Firmware (sketch.ino)

**Step 1:** Click the **`sketch.ino`** tab in the file panel.

**Step 2:** Select all existing content (`Ctrl+A`) and delete it.

**Step 3:** Open `Simulation/sketch.ino` from this repository, copy the entire contents, and paste it into the editor.

**Step 4:** Press **`Ctrl+S`** to save.

---

## 5. Installing Required Libraries

The sketch requires two libraries: **Adafruit SSD1306** and **Adafruit GFX Library**.

**Step 1:** In the Wokwi editor, click the **Library Manager** icon (book icon) in the left toolbar, or press **`F1`** and search for "Add Library".

**Step 2:** In the library search box, type **`Adafruit SSD1306`**.

**Step 3:** Click the **"+"** button next to `Adafruit SSD1306` to add it to your project.
> *Wokwi will automatically add the dependency `Adafruit GFX Library` as well.*

**Step 4:** Verify that your project's `libraries.txt` (auto-generated by Wokwi) now contains:
```
Adafruit SSD1306
Adafruit GFX Library
```

> **Note:** If Wokwi does not auto-resolve the Adafruit GFX Library, repeat the search for `Adafruit GFX Library` and add it manually.

## 6. Running the Simulation

**Step 1:** Click the green **"▶ Start Simulation"** button at the top of the editor.

**Step 2:** Wait 2–3 seconds for the ESP32 to boot. You will see the splash screen on the OLED:
```
SMART ENERGY MONITOR
  Initialising...
```

**Step 3:** After 1.5 seconds the splash clears and the main display appears with live readings.

> *Screenshot description: The OLED shows "SMART ENERGY MONITOR" at the top, three metrics (V, I, P) on the second row, a large power number in the centre, "STATUS: SAFE" below it, and a partially filled horizontal bar at the bottom.*

## 7. Expected Output

### OLED Display (SAFE state — potentiometers at mid position)
```
SMART ENERGY MONITOR
────────────────────
V:120.0  I:5.00  P:600
      600.0W
    STATUS: SAFE
0 [████████░░░░░░░] 3C
```

### OLED Display (OVERLOAD state — potentiometers turned up)
```
SMART ENERGY MONITOR
────────────────────
V:220.0  I:0.90  P:198
      198.0W
  !! OVERLOAD !!        ← flashes (inverted every 500ms)
0 [████████████████] 3C
```

### Serial Monitor (115200 baud)
```
Smart Energy Monitor v1.0 — booting...
Display OK. Starting main loop.
voltage:119.97,current:4.99,power:598.85,alert:0
voltage:220.15,current:0.88,power:193.73,alert:1
```

### Physical indicators
| State | Green LED | Red LED | Buzzer |
|---|---|---|---|
| SAFE (power ≤ 150W) | ON | OFF | Silent |
| OVERLOAD (power > 150W) | OFF | ON | Sounding |

## 8. Interacting with the Simulation

### Adjusting voltage (Potentiometer 1)
- Click the potentiometer labelled **"pot1"** on the canvas
- Drag the blue handle clockwise to increase voltage, anti-clockwise to decrease
- Watch the `V:` reading on the OLED update in real time

### Adjusting current (Potentiometer 2)
- Click **"pot2"** and drag to adjust the simulated current (0–10A)
- Power = V × I will recompute immediately

### Triggering OVERLOAD
- Turn both potentiometers up until `P:` exceeds 150W
- Red LED lights, green LED extinguishes, buzzer sounds, OLED shows flashing `!! OVERLOAD !!`
- Turn either potentiometer back down to return to SAFE

### Viewing Serial Output
- Click **"Serial Monitor"** tab at the bottom of the Wokwi editor
- Data appears every 500ms in the format: `voltage:xxx.xx,current:x.xx,power:xxx.xx,alert:0or1`

## 9. Troubleshooting

### Error 1: "SSD1306 not found" in Serial Monitor / blank OLED

**Cause:** I2C address mismatch or wiring error in `diagram.json`.

**Fix:**
1. Open `diagram.json` and confirm the OLED component has `"i2cAddress": "0x3c"`.
2. Verify the connections: `esp:D21 → oled1:SDA` and `esp:D22 → oled1:SCL`.
3. Confirm `esp:3V3.1 → oled1:VCC` and `esp:GND.1 → oled1:GND`.
4. If still blank, try changing the I2C address to `"0x3d"` — some SSD1306 modules use this address.

---

### Error 2: LEDs not lighting / Buzzer silent

**Cause:** Missing resistor connections or incorrect pin mapping.

**Fix:**
1. Confirm `esp:D26 → r1:2 → ledG:A → GND` (green LED chain).
2. Confirm `esp:D27 → r2:2 → ledR:A → GND` (red LED chain).
3. Confirm `esp:D25 → bz1:1` and `bz1:2 → GND`.
4. Check that GPIO pin names in `diagram.json` match the `#define` values in `sketch.ino` (D26, D27, D25).
5. In Wokwi, LED polarity matters — Anode (`A`) connects toward the resistor (positive side); Cathode (`C`) connects to GND.

---

### Error 3: Power readings stay at 0W / ADC always reads 0

**Cause:** Potentiometer wiper not connected to the correct ADC pin, or potentiometer VCC/GND reversed.

**Fix:**
1. Confirm `pot1:SIG → esp:D34` and `pot2:SIG → esp:D35`.
2. Confirm each potentiometer has VCC connected to `esp:3V3` (not 5V) and GND to `esp:GND`.
3. In Wokwi, click the potentiometer and drag the handle — if the OLED value stays at 0, the SIG wire is missing.
4. Verify in `sketch.ino` that `PIN_VOLTAGE = 34` and `PIN_CURRENT = 35` match the wired pins.
5. ESP32 GPIO34 and GPIO35 are input-only — if you accidentally connected an output device to them, remove it.
